## Mathematics and path semantics

- The pose quaternion maps TCP coordinates to world. `relativeRotationVector(a,b)` computes the shortest body-frame logarithm of $q_a^{-1}q_b$. The right Jacobian implementation uses $J_r(\phi)=I-(1-\cos\theta)[\phi]_\times/\theta^2+(\theta-\sin\theta)[\phi]_\times^2/\theta^3$; its inverse has the corresponding positive half-cross term. Thus $q=q_A\exp(\phi)$ has body rate $J_r(\phi)\phi'$, and the cubic controls $0,h\omega_A/3,\Delta-hJ_r^{-1}(\Delta)\omega_B/3,\Delta$ match both cut rates. The noncommuting finite-difference test checks these signs and frames. Position remains C2; orientation is C1 at the two cuts, with no angular-acceleration continuity claim.
- LIN body angular rate is constant along its shortest SLERP. CIRC has two such rates, on start→via and via→end. The retained via pose is a required sample outside the local cut; an angular-rate jump at that original via remains. Exact 180° endpoint ties still depend on the input quaternion sign. A rotation-vector cubic can travel farther than the endpoint geodesic because it also matches the incoming and outgoing rates; the note states this distinction.
- The derivative control hull bounds $\|\phi'\|$, and $\|J_r\|\le1$ bounds declared body angular rate. The global quintic clock contributes a maximum normalized rate of $1.875/T$, so the duration floor $1.875L\omega_{\ell,\max}/\omega_{\max}$ is conservative for the **declared orientation reference**. During review I found that sampling retained CIRC rates at fractions $10^{-9}$ from an endpoint could omit a very short via-side segment. The planning agent changed enumeration to `angularVelocityAt(0)` and `(1)` for each path; CIRC returns its first and second rates at those exact fractions. I verified the current code and reran the tests.
